# HR Analytics Dashboard | MySQL and Power BI

## Project Overview

The project involves the use of SQL and Microsoft Power BI to analyse HR data with a view to examining workforce distribution, employee attrition, overtime, job satisfaction, performance ratings, monthly income, and training hours.

The goal is to convert HR data into useful insights which will enable an organisation to understand workforce trends and spot areas that might need further investigation.

## Tools and Technologies

* MySQL provides the facilities for creating a database, creating tables, querying data, performing aggregations, applying filters, and carrying out business analysis.
* Microsoft Power BI includes KPI cards, interactive slicers, charts, and a matrix visual.
* SQL: SELECT, WHERE, GROUP BY, ORDER BY, COUNT, SUM, CASE WHEN, AVG, and ROUND.

## Business Questions ans Insight

**1. How many employees are in each department, classified by roles?**
The results show that no single department accounts for a disproportionately large share of the workforce. Employee distribution ranges from 19.1% to 20.6%, indicating a relatively balanced workforce structure.

**Business insight:**
The company might use this information to compare the staffing levels and workloads of different departments. Even though the distribution is generally balanced, when deciding on staffing it is necessary to take into account the differences in workload, productivity, and employee demand.

**2. How many employees are aged 59 or older in each department?**
The analysis identified the following employees aged 59 or older:
HR	87
Sales	77
Finance	76
Marketing	76
IT	73

HR has the highest number of employees aged 59 or older, while IT has the lowest.

**Business insight:**
The number of older employees can help the organisation with workforce and succession planning. The company should consider its retirement policies and potential future vacancies when planning recruitment, promotions, skills transfer, and succession. The results do not mean that these employees will necessarily retire soon. Retirement timing will depend on individual circumstances and company policy.

**3. How does job satisfaction differ across performance ratings?**
The analysis compared job satisfaction ratings from 1 to 5 with performance ratings from 1 to 5.
The results show that employees with different performance levels also have different levels of job satisfaction. There are employees with high performance but low satisfaction, as well as employees with low performance and high satisfaction.

**Business insight:**
Performance and job satisfaction should not be treated as the same measure. Employees with low performance may benefit from training, coaching, clearer expectations, or additional support. At the same time, employees with low job satisfaction should be investigated to understand potential factors affecting their experience.

The company could use anonymous employee surveys to identify factors influencing satisfaction. It could also examine employees with both high performance and high satisfaction to understand what workplace factors may be contributing to positive employee experiences.

**4. What percentage of overtime do the various departments have?**
Overtime is relatively balanced across the departments, with an overall overtime rate of approximately 48.2%. The results suggest that overtime is not concentrated in one specific department.

**Business insight:**
The company should monitor overtime to understand workload, staffing requirements, productivity, and employee well-being. Rather than simply increasing overtime, management could investigate whether consistently high overtime indicates understaffing or workload pressures.

**5. Which departments have the highest employee attrition?**
Attrition is approximately 50% across all departments, indicating that employee turnover is not isolated to one department.

**Business insight:**
The sales department has the highest rate of staff turnover, whereas the marketing department has the lowest. Nevertheless, the difference between the departments is fairly small. The company should look into the reasons for employees leaving by considering factors like job satisfaction, overtime, income, opportunities for career advancement, workload, training opportunities, and working conditions. Further insights into the causes of turnover could be obtained through employee surveys or exit interviews.

**6. What is the difference in average monthly income among various job roles?**
Managers have the highest average monthly income, followed by Team Leaders. Executives and Analysts have similar average income levels, while Interns have the lowest average income.

**Business insight:**
The differences in average income reflect the different job levels and responsibilities within the organisation. This analysis can help the company compare compensation across roles and identify areas that may require further investigation, particularly when considering employee retention, career progression, and internal pay structures.
